Tx (Transmitter) limits depend on the measurement reference point, clock architecture, rate, and test pattern. Begin with the path in Figure 8-1, then connect voltage, equalization, and jitter to the applicable conditions.

Connect the accessible point to the specified reference plane

In Figure 8-1 locate the DUT (Device Under Test) Tx pins, the Breakout Channel leading to connectors, and accessible point TP1. A waveform captured at TP1 includes that channel's effects.

Next locate the Replica Channel and its TP2/TP3 ends. It reproduces the breakout path's electrical characteristics to help refer measurements to the pins; it is not another series path traversed by Tx data. Section 8.3.1.1 de-embeds voltage parameters to pins unless stated otherwise.

De-embedding removes measurement-path effects using channel characteristics. It is not subtraction of a fixed voltage or jitter value. Section 8.3.5.2 also explains high-frequency noise amplification and the applicable processing limits.

Record the DUT/Lane, rate, full/reduced swing, pattern, clock architecture, measurement point, and processing before comparing a report with a parameter limit.

Identify the voltage quantity before looking up its limit

Read §8.3.2, then Figure 8-3. D+ and D− are individual single-ended voltages. Subtract them for differential voltage and average them for common mode.

VDIFF = VD+ − VD−; VCM = (VD+ + VD−) / 2. Peak-to-peak voltage, written mVPP here, is the maximum minus minimum of the same quantity. Equation 8-1's VDIFFp-p = 2 × max|VD+ − VD−| specifically assumes a symmetric differential swing.

Figure 8-3 relates these waveforms; it does not let an arbitrary curve height stand in for a specified Tx measurement. Follow the applicable §8.3.3.6 or §8.3.3.7 method, then select the rate, parameter, and units in Table 8-6.

Why does 450 mVPP single-ended become 900 mVPP differential?

Simulated data: Author-created teaching data, not a device/platform measurement. Assume one Endpoint Tx Lane driving from the DUT toward TP1 at 5.0 GT/s, full swing, Non-Embedded Refclk, clean external clock, and no SRIS test. The chosen method is 1-port measurement with CC CDR. Assume the applicable compliance test load and the channel processing required for differential voltage.

Section 8.3.3.6 uses average transition eye amplitude at 0.5 UI over 500 Compliance Pattern repetitions at 2.5/5.0 GT/s. The following are idealized representative levels under that assumption; they explain amplitudes without modeling edges, overshoot, or noise. UI (Unit Interval) supplies the time unit for that sampling location.

Complementary representative stateD+ (mV)D− (mV)VDIFF (mV)VCM (mV)
H825375+450600
L375825−450600

State H gives 825 − 375 = +450 mV; state L gives 375 − 825 = −450 mV. Differential peak-to-peak is therefore +450 − (−450) = 900 mVPP. Each individual conductor varies from 375 to 825 mV, only 450 mVPP single-ended. Both states give 600 mV common mode, an average potential rather than differential swing.

Follow Note 3 back to the method

In Table 8-6 choose VTX-DIFF-PP and the 5.0 GT/s column. Full-swing differential peak-to-peak voltage is 800–1000 mVPP. Note 3 points to §§8.3.3.6 / 8.3.3.7. Compare 900 mVPP from this case, rather than the 450 mVPP of D+ alone.

Under the stated measurement assumptions, that one swing result is in range. Jitter, common-mode noise, equalization, and other requirements need their own measurements. Two representative levels yielding 600 mV do not prove that a complete waveform has no AC common-mode noise.

Reduced-swing operation selects the separate VTX-DIFF-PP-LOW row. Both rows have a 1000 mVPP maximum in this edition, but different minima. A copied upper limit loses that mode distinction.

Change methods when the rate and preset change

At 2.5/5.0 GT/s Tx equalization is de-emphasis, measured using the specified transition/non-transition average eye-amplitude ratio in §§8.3.3.1 / 8.3.3.4. Higher rates use coefficients and presets (predefined equalization combinations). Two representative levels from the preceding case are not a complete preset test.

For swing at 8.0/16.0/32.0/64.0 GT/s, §8.3.3.7 sets the applicable precursor/postcursor coefficients to zero and uses a low-frequency Compliance Pattern segment: 64 zeros then 64 ones at 8.0/16.0/32.0 GT/s, or 64 PAM4 level-0 then 64 level-3 symbols at 64.0 GT/s. Measure the specified late portion of each half-cycle and average 500 repetitions.

For the preset itself, Figure 8-7 compares measured equalized output with a reconstruction made by applying coefficients to measured unequalized output, finding the best fit. Table 8-1 applies to 8.0/16.0/32.0 GT/s; Table 8-2 applies to 64.0 GT/s. Their ratios and tolerances define presets; coefficient magnitude is not output-voltage magnitude.

Finally check the reference plane for the actual item. Table 8-6 explicitly says that VTX-AC-CM-PP and VTX-AC-CM-PP-filtered noise analysis is done without de-embedding. These common-mode voltage parameters are not jitter, and are an exception to the general voltage-processing rule. A report saying “everything referred to pins” still needs a row-by-row applicability check.
