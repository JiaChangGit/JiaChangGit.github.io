What remains after seeing a valid TS1 or TS2?

One valid sequence is one successful reception. Transition rules also depend on consecutive reception, transmission counts, and the Lane set. TS1 and TS2 are Training Sequence Ordered Sets. Each end transmits its own sequences and independently checks received content.

Section 4.2.6 gives LTSSM timeouts a −0 / +50% tolerance unless stated otherwise. Nominal 24 ms and 48 ms therefore have upper limits of 36 ms and 72 ms. Do not require every implementation to transition at exactly 24.000 ms or 48.000 ms. Establish the timer origin, observation time, and continuity of the record.

The Polling.Active timeout path uses “any Lane” and an implementation-specific predetermined Lane set, unlike the normal all-detected-Lanes condition. It permits further training without identifying a broken Lane. Polling.Compliance can also follow Enter Compliance or specified training-control conditions; recover the actual control and received sequence history.

Why can Link Training be zero during Polling?

Link Training in Link Status describes selected conditions, not every LTSSM state. This case reads the Root Port. The bit is inapplicable and hardwired to zero in Endpoints, Switch Upstream Ports, and PCI Express to PCI/PCI-X Bridges.

Root Port progressLink TrainingInterpretation
Polling0Training sequences are still exchanged; zero does not imply L0
Initial Configuration1Polling has ended; width, Lane, and subsequent training remain
L00History and other states distinguish this zero from the earlier Polling zero

Follow Recovery when increasing speed

Initial training reaches L0 at 2.5 GT/s even when both ends support higher rates. A later speed change uses Recovery from L0. Advertised higher rates during Polling are not the current signaling rate. Section 4.2.7.2.4 explicitly makes Polling.Speed unreachable.

Equalization (per-Lane transmitter/receiver adjustment to compensate for the channel) has rate-specific procedures. Section 4.2.4 requires the applicable procedure for the first transition to each rate at or above 8.0 GT/s, while defining jointly supported/advertised bypass mechanisms. Separate the normal procedure, bypass to the highest NRZ rate, and No Equalization Needed. One end's bit does not justify skipping work across the whole path.

Connect the requesting end, Recovery substates, applicable equalization, and final speed/width. The two-ended Register and state walkthrough in Chapter 8 supplies the lookup entry. Recovery alone does not imply re-enumeration or clearing every Data Link state; actual LinkUp and Reset history matter.

Check power and test branches by their purpose

L0p (partial-Lane power saving within L0) is Flit-only, targets a symmetric width, and keeps at least one active Lane per direction. It does not first leave L0 to rebuild the entire Link; the two ends can briefly use different widths during a transition. L0s is a separate fast power state and must not be supported/advertised in Flit Mode, on Retimer Links, or with separate reference clocks and independent SSC (Spread Spectrum Clocking).

Polling.Compliance serves testing; the Loopback Lead requests the test and the Follower returns data; Hot Reset uses in-band signaling to reset a configured Link and associated downstream device. Figure 4-48 has distinct branches for them. A trace with “no TLP” still needs the state and entry conditions to distinguish training, power saving, and testing. See Chapter 9 for L1, Function power states, and platform policy.
