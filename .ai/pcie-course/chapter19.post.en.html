The LTSSM (Link Training and Status State Machine) connects termination detection, training, normal operation, and recovery. Identify each stage’s task before reading its substates and transition rules. A training wait alone does not establish a power failure.

Place an observation in the state diagram

Locate the observed LTSSM (link training state machine) state in Figure 4-48, read its task in section 4.2.6, then its detailed rules in 4.2.7. In the author scenario, a link reaches L0 and enters Recovery for a planned rate increase. Follow the speed-change and alignment conditions, then check the resulting rate and width; the state name alone is not a failure diagnosis.

Connect equalization to the electrical requirement

Equalization adjusts transmitter and receiver settings per lane. Start with the initiation rules in section 4.2.4, follow the rate-specific exchange, then check Chapter 8 electrical limits. The first change to each rate at or above 8.0 GT/s requires the procedure unless the specified No Equalization Needed conditions apply to all components. Completion of the procedure does not remove electrical requirements.

Add retimer roles without inventing a switch

A Retimer is a Physical-Layer-protocol-aware extension device with two Pseudo Ports. Figure 4-61 identifies the segments. It usually forwards traffic but executes specified functions such as equalization phases 2/3. A Retimer (signal retiming device) is transparent to the Data Link and Transaction Layers; it is not an extra bus-numbered switch. Base 6.0 permits at most two per link, subject to additional form-factor rules, and software must not enable L0s on such links.

Distinguish power and test branches

L0p (partial-lane power saving within L0) is Flit-only, symmetric in width, and keeps at least one active lane in each direction. L0s is a separate fast power state and must not be supported/advertised for Flit Mode or retimer links. Compliance and loopback branches serve testing, rather than mandatory steps in ordinary device operation.
