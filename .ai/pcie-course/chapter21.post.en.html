Rx (Receiver) operates on the signal after the channel. Receiver capability must be evaluated with channel loss, equalization, and Refclk (Reference Clock) conditions. A transmitter waveform alone does not characterize the complete signal path.

Start from the receiver reference point

A receiver stress test uses a calibrated channel approximating the specified worst-case loss. Section 8.4.1 defines the recovered eye at the receiver latch input: at 2.5/5.0 GT/s it corresponds to the Rx pins, while higher rates use the signal after behavioral receiver equalization. In the author scenario, a closed raw eye is not itself a receiver failure result; evaluate the defined recovered-eye reference and conditions.

Establish the channel boundary

Section 8.5.1 distinguishes captive channels from cases governed by the appropriate form-factor specification, which takes precedence in that split-channel situation. Figures 8-56 and 8-57 connect transmitter, channel, and receiver models. Keep reference position, frequency/time conditions, model, and notes with each parameter rather than comparing isolated loss values.

Use the clock architecture’s own model

Section 8.6.4 describes Common Refclk using topology and jitter transfer functions. It does not define corresponding transfer functions and jitter limits for Independent Refclk there; implementers trade reference jitter and circuit characteristics to meet transmitter requirements. Section 8.6.5 separates measurement edge filtering from modeled clock/receiver filtering. The models do not mandate a particular PLL (Phase-Locked Loop) or CDR (Clock and Data Recovery) implementation.
