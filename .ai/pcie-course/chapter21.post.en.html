Rx (Receiver) operates on the signal after the channel. Receiver capability must be evaluated with channel loss, equalization, and Refclk (Reference Clock) conditions. A transmitter waveform alone does not characterize the complete signal path.

Separate the calibrated eye from the receiver result

Rx (Receiver) testing first establishes a prescribed stressed input, then tests the actual receiver. A stressed eye combines specified channel loss, jitter, and interference. Successful calibration prepares the stimulus; it does not supply the DUT (Device Under Test) result.

Section 8.4.1 defines the recovered eye at the receiver latch input. At 2.5/5.0 GT/s this corresponds to Rx pins. At 8.0/16.0/32.0/64.0 GT/s it corresponds to the Rx die-pad signal after behavioral equalization. Measurement plus models constructs this inaccessible reference point.

The worked case uses 16.0 GT/s and builds on the preceding chapter's voltage/reference-plane explanation.

Keep clock architecture in the analysis

Refclk (Reference Clock) is more than a connection on a drawing. Common Refclk in §8.6.6 distributes one source to Tx and Rx, so some low-frequency movement propagates jointly. Relative timing at the latch also depends on Tx/Rx PLLs (Phase-Locked Loops), CDR, and transport-delay differences.

In Figure 8-89 locate the common source branches and the difference between transmitted data and receiver sampling-clock paths. Section 8.6.5 separates edge filtering, which reduces measurement-sampling artifacts, from behavioral filtering by PLLs, CDR, and applicable transport delay. Their combination yields effective Refclk jitter at the latch rather than a simple sum of raw jitter.

Section 8.6.4 does not define matching Refclk transfer functions and jitter limits for Independent Refclk (IR) there; other transmitter and receiver requirements still apply. Changing this case to IR requires a new Sj profile/model selection rather than reusing CC Figure 8-45. See Chapter 20 for the already explained Tx clock-sampling table.

Refclk testing in §8.6.1 tests the clock source, while the worked case tests the Rx. Keep each test's reference point and result separate before connecting them into one analysis.
