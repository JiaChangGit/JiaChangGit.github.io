TC (Traffic Class) labels a packet; VC (Virtual Channel) associates traffic with queues, buffers, and flow-control resources. A higher TC (traffic label) does not by itself reserve bandwidth. First follow the mapping on one link, then examine arbitration and system-wide service.

Follow the map over each link

Table 2-43 gives an example with TC0–TC6 mapped to VC0 and TC7 to VC1. Figure 2-88 places the map at link ends. A VC (Virtual Channel, traffic-resource association) adds neither physical lanes nor link speed. Switch ports support independent mapping, so verify both ends of each link rather than assuming one global map.

Separate blocking from a latency guarantee

Author scenario: in Non-Flit Mode, after matching maps are configured, VC0 lacks receive credits while VC1 has sufficient resources. An otherwise eligible TC7 packet can still participate in transmission arbitration. It must still wait if its own credits are unavailable, it is not scheduled, or the shared link is busy. Isolation removes one source of blocking; it does not establish a latency bound.
