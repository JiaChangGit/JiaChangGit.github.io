FC (Flow Control) tracks buffer space at the adjacent receiver. Link replay handles transmission integrity, while a Completion (transaction response) belongs to the request itself. We place all three along one path so their participants and guarantees stay clear.

Follow the FC state; the DL_Up output alone does not establish permission to transmit.

Why can a smaller UpdateFC value provide more available credit?

CREDIT_LIMIT is the cumulative limit advertised by the receiver; CREDITS_CONSUMED is the transmitter's cumulative use. Both wrap at their field widths. The latest UpdateFC value alone is not the available balance.

What do Ack and UpdateFC change after normal transmission?

The new TLP consumes 1 PH and 2 PD once when it passes the FC gate. Consumed PH becomes FFh; consumed PD wraps from FFEh plus 2 to 000h. The available balance becomes 1 PH / 0 PD. A later valid Ack permits release of acknowledged replay copies but does not change the FC counters.

When the receiver processes the data and makes the corresponding space available, it advances CREDITS_ALLOCATED and advertises Update B. Only after the transmitter receives that valid update does its limit become PH=01h, PD=002h, restoring 2 PH / 2 PD. Section 2.6.1.1 expressly forbids interpreting credit return as transaction completion or system visibility.

How does a zero UpdateFC differ from Infinite Credit?

The initialization advertisement 08h/008h established finite accounting here; subsequent zeros remain cumulative-counter values. Infinite Credit is a different state established by its initialization encoding. It prevents throttling for that credit type, while other transmission conditions remain. It does not imply an infinite physical receive buffer.

Does Replay consume a second set of credits?

No. In an alternative to the normal example, suppose the first transmission fails LCRC and a Nak causes replay. The Retry Buffer resends the same TLP, which already passed the FC gate. Section 2.6 states that FC accounting is unaware of repeated Data Link transmissions.

Replay itself leaves CREDITS_CONSUMED at PH=FFh, PD=000h, without consuming another 1 PH and 2 PD. A valid concurrent UpdateFC can still change CREDIT_LIMIT. Section 3.6.2.1 also explains that Retry Buffer entries have already been allocated receive space by the transmitter's FC logic. A new write generated by the upper layer is a different TLP and needs a new credit check and consumption.

Neither Ack nor UpdateFC substitutes for Completion. The example's Posted Memory Write has no ordinary Completion. For a Memory Read, NPH covers the Request, while CplH/CplD are checked in the return direction. The requester verifies Requester ID, Tag, Status, and returned data coverage. See Chapter 5 for matching and completeness, and Chapter 8 for detailed Replay rules.

LCRC (Link Cyclic Redundancy Check) and sequence numbers protect an adjacent Link. ECRC (End-to-End CRC) adds integrity checking across intermediate components under its support/enable conditions. Data Poisoning marks known-unreliable data without repairing it. These mechanisms address different responsibilities from receive-buffer accounting.

What changes with Scaled FC or Flit Mode?

Scaled FC (Scaled Flow Control) activation is established through Data Link Feature Exchange; initialization supplies HdrScale/DataScale. Table 2-47 changes effective counter widths with scaling. The example's fixed 8-bit/12-bit modular arithmetic cannot simply be retained.

Flit Mode adds Shared FC and dedicated credits, with additional rules for Completion merging, accounting granularity across contiguous TLPs, and the applicable gate. One transmitted Flit does not mean one credit.

Protection also moves to Flit-level CRC, FEC (Forward Error Correction), and Replay. Section 3.1 calls the Flit link CRC LCRC; section 3.6.2.3 (p.300) explicitly excludes Ack/Nak DLLPs in Flit Mode. Do not copy the example's return-packet formats into a Flit trace. Continue to Chapter 18 for full Flit carriage and protection.

Why can the transmitter wait after receive space is freed?

Separate three events: the receiver processes TLPs and advances CREDITS_ALLOCATED; it schedules and sends UpdateFC; the transmitter receives a valid update and changes CREDIT_LIMIT. The first can finish while later events remain pending. An update whose DLLP integrity check fails does not take effect.

The section 2.6.1.2 Non-Flit Mode Flow Control Update Latency note measures from freed receive space to the first Symbol transmitted for the corresponding UpdateFC. Tables 2-48–2-50 are recommended latency guidelines, not the entire transmission-plus-processing delay. Required update-scheduling triggers and periodic-update rules must be read separately.

Appendix H explains the derivation and is explicitly informational. For persistently zero PD, locate the missing event along that chain. A wait caused by Replay, ordering, or arbitration belongs to its own mechanism.
