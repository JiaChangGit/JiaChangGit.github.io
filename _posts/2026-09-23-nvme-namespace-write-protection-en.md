<section id="topic-overview" class="topic-overview">
<p class="reader-paragraph opening"><span class="paragraph-number" aria-label="00.01">00.01.</span><span class="paragraph-text">A completed dataset may need to remain readable while later commands are prevented from modifying its namespace. Namespace Write Protection provides reversible, until-power-cycle and permanent states, separating hardware support, entry permission and actual protection. This report connects those distinctions to FID 84h and explains their consequences for command processing.</span></p><dl class="term-note" aria-label="Terms in this passage"><div><dt>Namespace Write Protection</dt><dd>A mechanism controlling whether a namespace accepts operations restricted by its protection state.</dd></div><div><dt>namespace</dt><dd>Namespace, a formatted quantity of non-volatile memory accessed by a host through a controller.</dd></div><div><dt>FID</dt><dd>Feature Identifier: selects the Feature to read or configure.</dd></div></dl>
<h2 id="main-ideas">The main ideas</h2>
<div class="topic-map">
<article><span class="axis-number">01</span><h3>Object and enforcement boundary</h3><p class="axis-paragraph"><span class="axis-paragraph-number" aria-label="01-01">01-01</span><span class="paragraph-text">The setting belongs to a namespace and is enforced by every attached controller. Distinguish the storage object from the command’s controller, then consider operations affecting multiple namespaces.</span></p><dl class="term-note" aria-label="Terms in this passage"><div><dt>controller</dt><dd>Controller, the entity that implements the NVMe interface, fetches commands, and reports completions.</dd></div></dl></article>
<article><span class="axis-number">02</span><h3>Track states through events</h3><p class="axis-paragraph"><span class="axis-paragraph-number" aria-label="02-01">02-01</span><span class="paragraph-text">Each state has specific entry and exit paths. Set Features, controller reset and power cycling are distinct triggers, so read every arrow together with its event.</span></p></article>
<article><span class="axis-number">03</span><h3>From capability to successful configuration</h3><p class="axis-paragraph"><span class="axis-paragraph-number" aria-label="03-01">03-01</span><span class="paragraph-text">NWPC advertises support, WPC permits particular entry requests, and WPS reports current state. Successful protection also commits existing volatile write-cache contents to media.</span></p><dl class="term-note" aria-label="Terms in this passage"><div><dt>NWPC</dt><dd>Namespace Write Protection Capabilities, a controller bit field advertising supported protection states.</dd></div><div><dt>WPC</dt><dd>Write Protection Control, the field permitting requests to enter particular protection states.</dd></div><div><dt>WPS</dt><dd>Write Protection State, the namespace’s current protection-state code.</dd></div></dl></article>
<article><span class="axis-number">04</span><h3>Use and observe protected storage</h3><p class="axis-paragraph"><span class="axis-paragraph-number" aria-label="04-01">04-01</span><span class="paragraph-text">Read, Flush and management commands have different rules, including cross-namespace effects. Current WPS, completion status and health warnings answer different questions.</span></p></article>
</div>
<p class="reader-paragraph"><span class="paragraph-number" aria-label="00.02">00.02.</span><span class="paragraph-text">Assumes operating systems, computer organization and basic SSD concepts. Namespaces 7/8 and controllers A/B are illustrative rather than assumed device configurations.</span></p>
<p class="reader-paragraph"><span class="paragraph-number" aria-label="00.03">00.03.</span><span class="paragraph-text">Start with the four states, examine support and control bits, complete a configuration example, and apply the result to commands. Related fields are grouped and linked from the main explanations.</span></p>
<div class="overview-connections"><h3>Connecting the main ideas</h3>
<p class="reader-paragraph"><span class="paragraph-number" aria-label="00.04">00.04.</span><span class="paragraph-text">Follow the dataset in namespace 7: choose a protection and release policy, verify that the transition is available, wait for successful configuration, and explain which operations remain possible. Each field answers a concrete question.</span></p>
<p class="reader-paragraph"><span class="paragraph-number" aria-label="00.05">00.05.</span><span class="paragraph-text">Keep three distinctions in view: support versus entry permission, non-saveability versus persistence, and namespace protection versus an all-media health warning.</span></p>
</div>

<details class="review-question" id="qa-nwp-reset"><summary>1. After a controller reset without power cycling, can Set WPS=0 release WPS=2?</summary>
<div data-qa-answer="nwp-reset"><p class="reader-paragraph review-answer"><span class="paragraph-number" aria-label="08.01">08.01.</span><span class="paragraph-text">No. That reset retains state 2, which Set cannot change. A power cycle follows the special return arrow to 0. Clearing WPUPCC closes future entry permission; it does not release existing protection.</span></p></div>

<details class="review-question" id="qa-nwp-capability"><summary>3. If a successful capability query returns DW0=6, is WPS=6 and can permanent protection be released?</summary>

<details class="review-question" id="qa-nwp-flush"><summary>4. Why does Flush succeed without writing data after protection?</summary>
<div data-qa-answer="nwp-flush"><p class="reader-paragraph review-answer"><span class="paragraph-number" aria-label="08.04">08.04.</span><span class="paragraph-text">The transition already commits all associated volatile write-cache data and metadata. Success here does not imply another write, and the rule cannot be extended to Flush on an unprotected namespace.</span></p></div>
